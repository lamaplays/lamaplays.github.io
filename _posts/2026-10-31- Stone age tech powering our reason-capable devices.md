Who doesnt remember or at least know the legendary battery life of old Nokia phones? Those beasts could go a week or more on a single charge, so what changes? exactly! *nothing*. our software and hardware capabilities exploded and we still use the same good'old techonolgy to power them, litheum batteries.

A lithium-ion (Li-ion) battery is a type of rechargeable battery that stores energy by moving lithium ions back and forth between two electrodes, slotting them into the solid materials on each side.[^1]

Lithium-Ions gave phones more power than they actually needed. Because the components didn't demand much, the battery easily won the race, resulting in a low power-consumption ratio and week-long charges.

But as devices continued following the exponantial moore's law, we continued using the same 35 years old tech to power it. Now don't get me wrong: Li ion batteries are indeed powerful, but they’ve fallen far behind in this race.

and in my opinon, its a about time to start addressing the energy density and longevity of Li ion as a new hungry user joined the playground! infact, experts have been looking at [alternatives](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1149/2.0481701jes) that could replace litheum ion in the near future.
